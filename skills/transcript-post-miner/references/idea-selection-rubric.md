# Idea Selection Rubric

Use this rubric to mine social post opportunities from long transcripts.

## Strong Ideas

A strong idea has at least three of these:

- Teaches a practical concept, workflow, framework, or decision rule.
- Is useful for data, ML, AI, MLOps, or software practitioners without full video context.
- Has enough evidence in the transcript to support a standalone post.
- Connects to real work: debugging, evaluation, design choices, trade-offs, reliability, cost, latency, tooling, hiring, or implementation.
- Can be stated as a clear claim, not just a topic label.
- Offers a concrete takeaway, checklist, anti-pattern, or example.
- Feels specific to this recording instead of generic AI commentary.

## Weak Ideas

Reject or downrank ideas that are mainly:

- Intro, agenda, housekeeping, Q&A logistics, or sponsor/context setup.
- A tool mention without a transferable lesson.
- A chapter summary rather than a post angle.
- Too dependent on screen-sharing details that cannot be understood from text.
- A broad topic already covered everywhere unless the transcript adds a specific view.
- Interesting only because of the video context, not as a standalone social post.

## Scoring

Use a 1-5 priority score:

- 5: Strong standalone educational post, clearly supported, likely good for LinkedIn, with a clear writing angle.
- 4: Good post candidate, may need light synthesis or careful framing.
- 3: Usable but not urgent, may work as a secondary post or X thread.
- 2: Thin or too context-dependent, keep only if the user needs many ideas.
- 1: Reject.

## Idea Types To Look For

- Misconception correction: "People often test agents like normal functions, but..."
- Decision framework: "Use this when choosing between A and B."
- Process walkthrough: "How to approach an open-ended AI engineering task."
- Production trade-off: reliability, cost, latency, evals, observability, retries, caching.
- Practical checklist: "Before scaling evals, check these things."
- Hiring/interview insight: how to scope, prioritize, explain decisions, or evaluate submissions.
- Tooling insight: when a tool helps and what it does not solve.
- Demo lesson: what the live build reveals that docs usually hide.

## Evidence Requirements

Each selected idea must include:

- At least one timestamped source moment.
- A short note explaining what that moment contributes.
- A confidence level: high, medium, or low.

Prefer ideas with two or three supporting moments when the strongest post angle spans multiple transcript sections.
