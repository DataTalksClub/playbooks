# DataTalks.Club Social Media

Use this reference for posts published by the DataTalks.Club LinkedIn or X account. Also read `datatalks-style.md` for the shared event-promotion voice and factuality rules, then read the relevant platform reference.

## Account Voice

Write as a community organization that connects a global audience of people working in data, ML, MLOps, AI, and software engineering. There is no single personality behind the account.

Prefer:

- Audience-centered framing: what practitioners are trying to learn, build, evaluate, deploy, debug, or operate.
- A concrete technical problem, useful distinction, learning opportunity, or community update.
- Clear language about what the event, course, module, speaker, or recording offers.
- Stories about a practitioner problem, a community learning moment, a workflow, or source-backed speaker expertise.
- `we` only when it clearly means DataTalks.Club is organizing, hosting, publishing, or sharing something.

Avoid:

- First-person singular.
- Personal anecdotes, opinions, relationships, or experiences attributed to an unnamed author.
- Phrases that imply Alexey is the narrator, such as `my friend`, `I invited`, or `I will show`, unless the post is explicitly a quotation with supplied attribution.
- Treating the audience as beginners by default; choose the technical depth from `audience.md` and the source.
- Sounding like a faceless corporate brand. Community-oriented means useful and inclusive, not formal or generic.

## Storytelling

DataTalks.Club posts may still have a narrative. Anchor it in one of these:

- A recognizable problem practitioners face.
- A transition in a learning path, such as moving from building a system to evaluating it.
- A failure mode or trade-off the event will examine.
- A question the community repeatedly encounters.
- A source-backed reason the speaker, workshop, module, or recording is useful.

Move from that context to the event naturally. Do not bolt event details onto an unrelated educational post.

## LinkedIn Shape

Use the space for enough context to make the post useful on its own. A substantial post will often contain:

1. A practical problem, question, or direct announcement.
2. A short explanation of why it matters to the audience.
3. The connection to the event, course, module, speaker, or recording.
4. A concise list of what people will learn, see, or revisit when supported by the source.
5. Event details and a clear CTA.

Not every post needs all five parts. Reminders should be shorter than announcements, and repurposed educational posts should lead with the idea rather than the event.

## X Shape

Preserve the same angle and facts as the LinkedIn version, but rebuild it for standard X limits:

- Use one focused idea per post.
- Always deliver DataTalks.Club X copy as a thread, including short announcements and reminders.
- Open with the audience problem, event topic, or direct announcement.
- Put the registration or recording link in the first or final post when possible.
- End a pre-event thread with the essential date/time and CTA; end a post-event thread with the recording, notes, or materials link.
- Do not fragment one sentence across posts merely to satisfy the limit.

## Final Check

- The post sounds like DataTalks.Club, not Alexey with pronouns changed.
- The value is clear to the intended audience segment.
- Any story is organizational, community-based, or practitioner-centered.
- LinkedIn has enough context without unnecessary length.
- X is a thread that follows `x.md`, and every post fits the applicable limit.
- The facts and CTA match the campaign source.
